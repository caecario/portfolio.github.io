Rebuilding store hiring from 42 days to 19

A recruitment process that everyone blamed on candidate supply, which turned out to be an approval problem.

Year
2025
Category
Recruitment
Scope
18 stores, 240 hires
My role
Process owner, end to end
Image slotimages/project-01.jpg
SituationWhere things stood

Average time to hire for store-level roles sat at 42 days against a 25-day target, and 4 of 18 stores were operating below minimum staffing for most of Q1. The standing explanation inside the business was that the labour market had tightened.

The data did not support that. Applications per vacancy had actually risen year on year. Candidates were not missing; they were being lost after they applied.

TaskWhat I owned

I was asked to bring time to hire under 25 days within two quarters, without lowering the hiring bar and without adding headcount to the recruitment team. Quality of hire, measured by 90-day retention, had to hold or improve.

ActionWhat I actually did

I timestamped every stage of 60 completed hires and found that 19 of the 42 days sat in two approval steps that had never rejected a single candidate. The bottleneck was structural, not behavioural.

  • Mapped the full funnel with timestamps per handoff, then shared the waiting-time breakdown with the store managers who owned each step.
  • Removed two approval loops that carried no decision, and delegated final sign-off for store-level roles to the store manager with a defined salary band.
  • Replaced free-form interviews with a four-competency scorecard so three different interviewers were judging the same thing.
  • Set a 48-hour service level between stages and published a weekly ageing report by store, so delay became visible to the person causing it.
  • Ran a two-hour calibration session with 22 hiring managers on how to score evidence rather than impressions.

The hardest part was not the redesign. It was persuading two regional managers to give up an approval right they read as a loss of authority, which I handled by showing them their own stores' vacancy cost per day.

ResultWhat changed

Time to hire reached 19 days by the end of the second quarter and held there for the following two. Retention improved rather than degraded, which mattered because speed gains that cost quality do not survive a year.
